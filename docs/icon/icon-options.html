The icon you have today, what it is trying to say, and five rounds of alternatives. Every option is shown at the sizes iPhone actually uses: the Home Screen (60 pt), Spotlight (40 pt) and Settings (29 pt). Refer to options by letter when you comment.

The current icon

It is one SVG, public/favicon.svg. A navy square holds an orange basketball with four seams. A navy disc sits in the middle of the ball with a white “5” on it: the next five players on the floor. The colors are the app’s own: the navy page background and the orange accent. npm run icons turns the SVG into the PNGs iPhone and Android use.

What I noticed:

  1. The disc covers the spot where the seams cross. At Home Screen size it looks more like a ring, a dial or a life buoy than a basketball.
  2. The seams are drawn slightly past the edge of the ball, so small nubs stick out at the top, bottom, left and right.
  3. The “5” is typed text in Helvetica Neue or Arial. The PNG generator uses whatever fonts are on the machine that runs it, so the 5 can change shape from one machine to the next. Drawing it as a shape fixes that. Options B–H do this.
  4. The SVG has its own rounded corners. iPhone cuts its own corner shape too, so the Home Screen PNG ends up with slightly darker corners. A full square with no corners is the cleaner source.

Round 4

Q, T, U and V, with new colors

Orange’s complement, its opposite on the color wheel, is blue. So every palette here pairs the app’s orange with a blue: the app’s navy, a richer royal blue, or a dark petrol blue-green. The glass chevron is brighter now, closer to G’s white arrow.

Round 3

L with a basketball

Each of these keeps L’s orange 5 and glass chevron and brings in the ball from B, C or M. Q–U sit on navy like L. T and V use M’s orange court. W puts L inside B’s ball.

Round 2

Liquid Glass and new ideas

Options I–M use the iOS 26 Liquid Glass look: frosted, see-through layers with a bright rim along the top-left edge and a soft shadow underneath. N–P are new ideas without glass.

A web app’s icon is a flat PNG, so the glass is part of the picture. It won’t catch the light or move when the phone tilts the way glass icons in Apple’s own apps do. If the Home Screen is set to Tinted or Clear, iOS may recolor it.
